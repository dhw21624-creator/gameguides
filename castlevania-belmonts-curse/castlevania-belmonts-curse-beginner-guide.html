Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Beginner Guide — Rose Belmont Combat & Arcana

Category: Beginner Guide • Updated: 2026-10-09 • Game: Castlevania: Belmont's Curse

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse releases October 15, 2026 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC, Switch and Switch 2 for $29.99. Developed as a direct follow-up to the lineage of Castlevania III and Curse of Darkness, it is a Metroidvania starring Rose Belmont, daughter of Trevor Belmont and Sypha Belnades, as she fights the night in Paris. The twist: Rose does not start with the famous Vampire Killer whip. She begins with a sword and a wide range of weapons, with the whip acquired later in the journey. This beginner guide covers her combat, loadout system, dodging, and the arcana magic she inherits from her mother.

Short version: Rose starts with a sword, not the whip. Swap loadouts in the Armoury, upgrade damage through equipment and levels (most enemies take 2+ hits), and master backdash, backflip, and slide dodging. Magic comes from arcana/tarot cards learned from Sypha's teachings.

Rose Belmont: Sword First, Whip Later

Series veterans will notice the change immediately: Rose Belmont does not begin with the Vampire Killer. She starts with a sword and has access to many weapons from early on, making her combat more flexible — and less whip-reliant — than the classic Belmont experience. The whip is still there, but it is a mid-game acquisition tied to the story, not your starting tool.

For the opening hours, the sword is your workhorse: decent speed, reliable reach, and forgiving on whiffs. As you collect new weapons, experiment with their movesets rather than defaulting to the highest damage number — different enemies and bosses respond better to different tools.

The Armoury: Swapping Loadouts

The Armoury menu is where you manage weapons, equipment, and loadouts. You can swap loadouts freely (outside combat), letting you prepare for specific areas or bosses — a fast weapon for corridors, a heavy weapon for tanky enemies, a ranged option for flying foes.

Beginner tips for the Armoury:

  • Check new weapons after every boss or zone clear; equipment upgrades are often hidden in chests and optional rooms.
  • Keep at least one ranged option equipped for enemies on ledges or above you — early Metroids-style bosses punish melee-only loadouts.
  • Do not over-focus on one stat; a balanced loadout survives more situations than a min-maxed one during a first playthrough.

Leveling & Stats: Upgrade Damage Early

Belmont's Curse uses a leveling and stats system alongside equipment. Levels raise base stats — attack, defense, magic, and vitality — and most enemies take 2+ hits to kill at baseline. That means damage upgrades matter enormously: faster kills mean fewer hits taken, less downtime, and smoother exploration.

Practical priority: upgrade damage via equipment and levels before defensive stats early on. In a Metroidvania, killing enemies faster solves most survivability problems — you take fewer hits because fights end sooner. Only pivot to defense when bosses or area hazards start one-shotting you.

Tip: Most enemies take 2+ hits even at a comfortable level. Do not be alarmed when basic foes survive a slash — upgrade damage and the kill speed improves quickly.

Dodging: Backdash, Backflip & Slide

Rose has three core movement-defensive tools, and knowing when to use each is the key to boss fights:

  • Standing backdash: a quick backward hop with brief invulnerability. Use it for tight windows and to reset spacing without losing your facing.
  • Double-tap backflip: a longer-distance evasion that carries you well out of danger. Use it for heavy telegraphed attacks and to reposition across the arena.
  • Slide (while moving): a low-profile dash that passes through enemies and projectiles. This is the game's best defensive tool once you master it — sliding through an enemy's attack to their blind side is a core tactic.

Practice against bosses. Each boss in Belmont's Curse has distinct attack patterns, and the game expects you to slide through, backflip over, or backdash out of specific moves. Watch the boss's tells for a few attempts before committing to a dodge style; most bosses have one or two attacks each answer well.

Arcana & Tarot Cards: Sypha's Magic

Rose's magic comes from arcana/tarot cards, inherited from her mother Sypha Belnades' teachings. Cards are found throughout the castle and can be equipped to grant active spells and passive bonuses. Think of them as the game's magic system: a fire card for damage, a barrier card for defense, a utility card for exploration — with room to customize your loadout as you collect more.

For a first playthrough, keep one offensive arcana card equipped at all times — magic is often the fastest answer to armored enemies and bosses with projectile phases. Experiment with new cards after each major zone; some of the best cards are rewards for exploring off the critical path.

Exploration Essentials: Paris by Night

Belmont's Curse is a Metroidvania set in Paris, and the map rewards thorough exploration:

  1. Check for breakable walls and ceilings — classic Castlevania secrets hide behind them.
  2. Return to early zones after gaining new abilities; the map opens up with backdash-improving upgrades and slide-capable terrain.
  3. Collect tarot cards and weapons hidden in optional rooms; several bosses are easier with specific loadouts.
  4. Use the slide to reach low passages and to cross hazard floors that would damage a walking Rose.
  5. Do not ignore side paths even if the main objective pulls you forward — many stat boosts live off the critical route.

Difficulty Options

If combat is too punishing, the game includes an easy difficulty option — no shame in using it. Easy mode reduces enemy damage and gives more forgiving damage windows, which is a great way to experience the castle and story first. You can always start a normal or higher run later once you know the map and boss patterns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does Rose ever get the Vampire Killer whip?

A: Yes. The whip is acquired later in the journey, but you are not locked into it — the Armoury lets you keep using your preferred weapon loadout.

Q: What is the best early weapon?

A: The starting sword is reliable. Upgrade damage through levels and equipment early, and experiment with new weapons from chests — some early finds outclass the starter significantly.

Q: How does arcana magic work?

A: Arcana/tarot cards are equipped as active spells and passive bonuses. You find them in the castle and customize your loadout in the Armoury menu.

Q: Is the game hard?

A: It is a classic Metroidvania — challenging but fair. An easy difficulty option exists for players who prefer the exploration and story.

Conclusion

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse honors the series' Metroidvania roots while giving Rose Belmont her own combat identity: sword-first, magic-armed, and whip-equipped later. Master the slide, upgrade damage early, collect tarot cards, and Paris by night will fall to the daughter of Belmont and Belnades.
