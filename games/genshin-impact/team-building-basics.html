Quick Answer: The 4-Role Framework

Most effective Genshin teams distribute these four roles across four characters (one character can fill multiple roles):

  1. On-field DPS (Driver): The character you spend the most time actively controlling. Deals damage with normal/charged attacks while triggering reactions.
  2. Off-field DPS (Sub-DPS): Deals damage from off-field through Elemental Skill/Burst that persist after swapping (e.g., Xingqiu, Fischl, Xiangling, Yelan).
  3. Support/Buffer: Amplifies team damage through buffs, resistance shred, or enabling reactions (e.g., Bennett, Kazuha, Sucrose, Zhongli).
  4. Sustain/Defense: Keeps the team alive through healing or shields (e.g., Bennett, Zhongli, Kokomi, Baizhu). Often overlaps with Support.

The difference between a random four characters and a proper team is often 5-10x damage output. Genshin isn't a game where you pick your favorites and hope for the best — elemental reactions multiply damage, and team synergy is the single biggest factor in clearing content. This guide teaches you the framework so you can build teams with any roster.

Understanding the 4 Roles

Genshin teams function on a "quickswap" design philosophy. You're not supposed to stay on one character permanently — you cycle through your team, casting skills and bursts, to maximize damage and energy generation.

1. On-Field DPS (Driver)

This character consumes the most field time. They either have high personal damage (Hypercarries like Hu Tao, Xiao, Neuvillette) or they're "drivers" who trigger off-field damage and reactions with their normal attacks (Tartaglia, Sucrose in Taser, Raiden in National). A good on-field DPS has: sustained damage output, synergy with off-field DPS elements, and some form of self-sufficiency (interruption resistance, self-healing, or mobility).

2. Off-Field DPS (Sub-DPS)

These characters deal damage while NOT on the field. Their Elemental Skill and/or Burst create persistent effects (rain swords, lightning strikes, pyro tornado) that continue dealing damage after you swap away. Key examples: Xingqiu (rain swords apply Hydro), Fischl (Oz deals Electro), Xiangling (Pyronado orbits and applies Pyro), Yelan (coordinated Hydro attacks), Nahida (Dendro application and linked damage).

The best off-field DPS characters apply their element frequently — this enables your on-field DPS to trigger reactions every second.

3. Support / Buffer

Buffers amplify your team's damage. Bennett (massive ATK buff + healing), Kazuha (Elemental DMG bonus + crowd control + resistance shred with Viridescent Venerer), Sucrose (Elemental Mastery sharing), Zhongli (universal resistance shred + unbreakable shield), Furina (team-wide DMG% buff with HP drain mechanic).

4. Sustain / Defense

Healers and shielders keep you alive. Zhongli (shield), Bennett (heal), Kokomi (heal), Baizhu (heal + shield), Jean (heal), Kuki Shinobu (heal, also enables Hyperbloom). Most teams want exactly one sustain slot. Some teams skip sustain entirely if they have enough interruption resistance and damage avoidance (rare, mostly speedrun comps).

Elemental Reactions: Your Damage Multiplier

Reactions are the core of Genshin's combat. Raw stats matter, but reactions multiply damage — a well-built reaction team often outperforms a pure stat-stick team 3:1.

Key Design Principle: The most powerful reaction teams (Vaporize, Melt, Hyperbloom) work because they use multiplicative scaling. Vaporize multiplies your existing damage by 1.5x (or 2x for reverse Vape). Hyperbloom damage scales purely with EM and character level — you build one stat on one character, and the cores do all the work. Choose your reaction FIRST, then pick characters that enable it.

Team Rotation Basics

A "rotation" is the sequence in which you use your characters' skills and bursts. A proper rotation:

  1. Starts with supports (buffs, shields, off-field deployables) — longest duration first
  2. Then off-field DPS (deploy their persistent damage)
  3. Then on-field DPS (use all the buffs and reactions)
  4. Repeat when buffs expire (usually every 15-20 seconds)

Example rotation (National team): Bennett Q → Xiangling Q (snapshots Bennett buff) → Xingqiu Q+E → on-field DPS normal attacks. The Pyronado keeps Bennett's ATK buff for its entire duration because Xiangling "snapshotted" it — this is a core mechanic for maximizing buff uptime.

Energy funneling: Use your support's skill, then immediately swap to the character who needs energy to "catch" the particles. This is critical for energy-hungry characters like Xiangling and Xingqiu.

Example Team Templates (Beginner-Friendly)

Template 1: Budget Hyperbloom (All 4-Stars)

  • On-field: Dendro Traveler (apply Dendro)
  • Off-field Hydro: Xingqiu or Barbara
  • Off-field Electro (Hyperbloom trigger): Kuki Shinobu (Full EM build)
  • Flex: Collei (more Dendro) or Sucrose (grouping + EM share)

Why it works: Hyperbloom damage scales ONLY with the Electro trigger's EM and level. Build Kuki full EM and level 90, and the Dendro cores will auto-target enemies for ~30k+ each. This team clears most content with minimal investment. It's the single most new-player-friendly team in the game.

Template 2: National Core (Flexible)

  • Core: Xiangling + Bennett + Xingqiu
  • Flex 4th slot: Raiden (Rational), Sucrose (Sucrose National), Kazuha, Tartaglia (International)

Why it works: Bennett buffs Xiangling's ATK, Xiangling snapshots the buff, Xingqiu provides Hydro for Vaporize. The core three alone handle everything. The 4th slot is pure upside. Build Xiangling with 4-piece Emblem of Severed Fate and as much ER as needed to Burst on cooldown (usually 180-220% depending on team).

Common Team Building Mistakes

  1. Too many on-field DPS: If you have Hu Tao, Xiao, and Wanderer on one team, two of them are doing nothing. You can only control one character at a time. Pick one on-field DPS and build around them.
  2. Ignoring energy requirements: A team that looks great on paper but nobody can Burst on cooldown is useless. Check ER requirements — Xiangling needs ~200% ER, Xingqiu needs ~200%, Yelan needs ~180%. Favonius weapons are team-wide energy batteries.
  3. No sustain: "I'll just dodge everything" works until it doesn't. One sustain slot (healer or shielder) prevents restarting Abyss chambers. Bennett counts as both buffer and sustain.
  4. Conflicting elements ruining reactions: Adding a Pyro character to a Freeze team melts the freeze. Adding Electro to a Vaporize team creates Overload instead. Reactions have priority — check the reaction order before adding a 4th slot.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I use four characters I like without worrying about synergy?

A: In the overworld, yes — almost anything works. In Spiral Abyss Floor 12 and high-difficulty content, no — you need synergy. Genshin's difficulty curve is gentle, but the endgame expects teams with proper reactions and roles. You can clear 95% of content with favorites. The last 5% needs synergy.

Q: What's the best team for a new player?

A: Hyperbloom with Kuki Shinobu (if you have her) or any variation of the National team (Xiangling + Bennett + Xingqiu). Both teams work with 4-star characters and scale extremely well into the endgame. They've been meta-relevant for years and will likely remain so.

Q: Do I need a 5-star to have a good team?

A: No. The strongest team cores in the game are mostly 4-stars: Xiangling, Bennett, Xingqiu, Fischl, Sucrose, Kuki Shinobu. These six characters form the backbone of meta teams. 5-stars are luxuries that make teams stronger or more comfortable, but they're never mandatory.

Q: How important is Elemental Resonance?

A: It's a bonus, not a requirement. Dual Pyro (+25% ATK) and Dual Hydro (+25% HP) are the most impactful. Dual Cryo (+15% CRIT vs frozen/cryo-affected) is strong in Freeze teams. Never force a bad character into your team just for resonance — good synergy always beats resonance.

Q: How do I know if my team is "good enough"?

A: Test it in Spiral Abyss or against a world boss without food buffs. If you can clear in under 90 seconds with clean rotations, your team works. If you're struggling, check: (a) are your reactions actually triggering, (b) is your rotation smooth with no energy gaps, (c) do you have a sustain character?
