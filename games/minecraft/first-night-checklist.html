Quick Answer: First Night in 60 Seconds

Punch 3 logs → craft planks → craft crafting table + sticks → craft wooden pickaxe → mine 20 cobblestone → craft stone tools + furnace → kill 3 sheep for wool → craft bed (or build dirt hut) → wait out the night. That's it. Now read on for the detailed breakdown.

Your first Minecraft day lasts exactly 10 real-world minutes before hostile mobs spawn at nightfall. This checklist walks you through every action in priority order so you never waste daylight. No prior experience needed — just follow along and you'll be safe before the first zombie groans.

Phase 1: First 60 Seconds — Wood & Crafting Table

You spawn in a random biome. Don't wander. Look around, spot the nearest tree, and start punching.

  1. Punch tree trunks until you have at least 3 oak logs (hold left-click). Any tree type works — oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia, dark oak, cherry, mangrove.
  2. Open inventory (E), place logs in the 2x2 crafting grid to make planks. 1 log = 4 planks.
  3. Craft a crafting table: fill all 4 slots of the 2x2 grid with planks.
  4. Place the crafting table on the ground (right-click a surface).
  5. Craft sticks: place 2 planks vertically in the crafting grid (1 above the other).
Pro Tip: If you spawn near a village, you can skip most of this checklist — villagers' beds, chests, and crafting tables give you a massive head start. But still gather wood for tools.

Phase 2: Minutes 1-3 — Stone Tools

  1. Use the crafting table to make a wooden pickaxe: 3 planks across the top row + 2 sticks down the middle.
  2. Find exposed stone — look for cliff faces, surface boulders, or dig down 3-4 blocks in any dirt area.
  3. Mine at least 20 cobblestone. This is your foundation for all stone tools and a furnace.
  4. Back at the crafting table, craft:
    • Stone pickaxe (3 cobblestone + 2 sticks)
    • Stone axe (3 cobblestone + 2 sticks, mirrored pattern)
    • Stone sword (2 cobblestone + 1 stick)
    • Furnace (8 cobblestone in a ring around the outer crafting slots)

Phase 3: Minutes 3-6 — Shelter & Bed

Night falls fast. You need two things: a shelter to block mobs and a bed to skip the night.

  1. Kill sheep. You need 3 wool of the same color. Find sheep (common in plains, forests, meadows) and punch them with your sword. Each sheep drops 1 wool.
  2. If no sheep nearby, collect string from spiders at dusk or break cobwebs in abandoned mineshafts (unlikely on day one). Otherwise plan for a no-bed night.
  3. Craft a bed: 3 wool across the middle row + 3 planks across the bottom row at the crafting table.
  4. Build a shelter. Minimum viable shelter:
    • Dig into a hill (3 blocks deep, 3 wide, 3 tall) — the classic "hobbit hole."
    • Or build a dirt box: 4 walls (3 blocks high), place blocks for a roof.
    • Place your bed inside.
    • Block the entrance with dirt (leave 1 block missing as a window to check for daylight).
Critical: Do NOT place your bed against an exterior wall. Mobs can reach through 1-block-thick walls and hit you while you sleep. Keep at least 1 block of space between bed and wall on all sides.

Phase 4: Minutes 6-9 — Food & Lighting

  1. Kill animals for food. Pigs, cows, chickens, rabbits — kill what you find. Raw meat restores hunger but cook it for better results.
  2. Make charcoal: Place 1 log in the top furnace slot and 1 wooden plank in the bottom fuel slot. 1 log = 1 charcoal.
  3. Craft torches: 1 stick + 1 charcoal (or coal if you found surface coal) = 4 torches. Make at least 8.
  4. Cook food: Place raw meat in the top furnace slot, planks/charcoal in the bottom. Cooked meat restores significantly more hunger.
  5. Place torches inside and around your shelter entrance to prevent mob spawns near your base.

Phase 5: Final Minute — Secure the Night

  1. Double-check: Is the entrance sealed? Are torches placed? Is the bed accessible?
  2. Right-click the bed to sleep. If it says "You can only sleep at night or during thunderstorms," wait until the sky darkens fully.
  3. If you couldn't craft a bed: use the furnace to smelt while you wait. Craft a wooden door for your entrance. Stand still and listen for mob sounds — zombies groan, skeletons rattle, spiders hiss, creepers are silent until they're close.
What to do at night with no bed: Smelt iron if you found any, organize your inventory, craft extra tools and torches, expand your shelter downward (start a mine). Never go AFK without blocking yourself in completely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What if I can't find sheep for a bed?

A: Kill spiders at dusk for string — 4 string can craft 1 wool block, but you need 12 string for 3 wool which is unlikely on day one. Better plan: build a sealed dirt shelter, craft a wooden door, place torches, and wait. Use the night to dig a mine staircase.

Q: Which biome is best for a first-night spawn?

A: Plains and meadows are ideal — abundant sheep, cows, pigs, exposed stone, and flat terrain. Forests give easy wood but reduced visibility. Deserts are challenging (no wood, scarce animals). Jungle and badlands are terrible for beginners.

Q: Can I skip the furnace and just eat raw food?

A: Yes, but cooked food restores roughly 2-3x more hunger points and more saturation. Cooking is worth the 10 seconds it takes. Raw chicken also has a 30% chance of giving you hunger (food poisoning), so always cook chicken.

Q: Should I explore or stay put on day one?

A: Stay put. Exploring is for day two and beyond. Day one is pure survival infrastructure: wood, stone, shelter, bed, food, torches. Once you have those, you can safely explore the next morning.

Q: What if I die on the first night?

A: You respawn at world spawn (or your bed if you slept). Your items drop where you died and despawn after 5 minutes of loaded chunk time. On your second attempt, speed through the checklist — you now know exactly what to do. If you made a bed and slept at least once, you'll respawn at the bed, which is much safer.

This guide reflects the Minecraft 1.21+ survival loop. Core first-night mechanics (wood → stone → shelter → bed) have been stable for years. Updated May 2026.
