Quick Answer: The Most Efficient Diamond Mining Method

Dig to Y=-57. Create a 2-block-tall, 1-block-wide main tunnel. Every 3 blocks, dig a 1x2 branch tunnel extending at least 100 blocks. Use a Fortune III pickaxe. This branch mining configuration yields the highest diamonds-per-hour in Minecraft 1.21 due to the post-1.18 ore blob sizes and reduced air exposure mechanic.

Diamond mining changed fundamentally in Minecraft 1.18. The old Y=11 meta is obsolete. Reduced air exposure means diamonds are rarer on cave walls, and the optimal Y-level is now far deeper. This guide covers every viable mining method ranked by efficiency, plus the tools and enchantments that maximize your yield.

Branch Mining (Best Method)

Branch mining at the correct spacing is the highest diamond-per-hour method in 1.21. Here's the exact setup:

  1. Dig a staircase from the surface to Y=-57. Use a 2x1 diagonal staircase (stand at the edge, dig the block beneath you and the block ahead, step down, repeat). This is safer and faster than a spiral staircase.
  2. Create a main tunnel: 2 blocks tall, 1 block wide, extending as long as you want. Place torches every 8-12 blocks.
  3. Branch spacing — every 3 blocks: From the main tunnel, dig a 1-wide, 2-tall branch tunnel perpendicular to the main tunnel. Leave 3 blocks between branches (not 2, not 4). The reasoning: in 1.18+, diamond blobs are small enough that 3-block spacing exposes every blob without wasted overlap. 2-block spacing wastes pickaxe durability on blocks you've already "seen" from the adjacent branch.
  4. Branch length: Extend each branch at least 100 blocks. Diamond veins average roughly 1 per chunk (16x16 blocks on the X/Z axes). Longer branches = more chunks crossed = more diamonds per setup time.
  5. Lighting: Place torches every 8 blocks in branches to prevent mob spawns behind you.
Pro Tip: Mine at head height (Y=-57) so your feet are at Y=-58. This places your eye level at the peak diamond concentration while keeping you above the worst bedrock interference.

Cave Mining Strategy

Post-1.18, caving for diamonds is less efficient than branch mining because of the reduced air exposure mechanic, but it still works — especially in large deepslate caves that already breach multiple chunks.

  • Focus on deepslate cave systems below Y=0. The deeper, the better.
  • Check the ceiling and walls, not just the floor — diamonds can generate on any exposed deepslate surface.
  • Use Night Vision potions to spot ores from a distance without placing torches.
  • Caving is better for iron and coal than diamonds; use it as a supplementary method.

Water Cave / Aquifer Mining

Deepslate aquifers (flooded caves below Y=0) are common in 1.21. Mining underwater is slower but has two advantages: (1) lava is instantly extinguished on contact, and (2) aquifers often expose large cave walls that cut through multiple chunks.

  • Use a Respiration III + Aqua Affinity helmet for normal-speed mining underwater
  • Place doors or magma blocks to create air pockets
  • Conduit power eliminates the need for air and gives night vision — worth setting up if you plan extended underwater mining

Tunnel Bore (Late Game)

A TNT or moss-based tunnel bore is the fastest diamond acquisition method in the game, but requires significant redstone and resource investment. TNT dupers (Java Edition only) push a flying machine that drops lit TNT, blasting a wide tunnel through deepslate and exposing every ore in its path. This is an endgame method — you need slime blocks, observers, and TNT duping knowledge. Not practical before you already have a solid diamond supply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many diamonds can I expect per hour of branch mining?

A: With an Efficiency V + Fortune III pickaxe at Y=-57, experienced miners report averages in the range of 30-60 diamond items per hour. This depends heavily on luck (chunk generation), but 30+ is a reasonable expectation for a dedicated mining session. Without Fortune III, expect roughly half that.

Q: Should I use a diamond or netherite pickaxe?

A: Netherite is better — it has higher durability (~2031 vs ~1561) and mines slightly faster. But a diamond pickaxe is perfectly fine. The real difference-makers are the enchantments (Efficiency V, Fortune III, Unbreaking III, Mending), not the base material.

Q: Does the biome affect diamond spawn rates?

A: No. Diamond generation is purely depth-based. A deepslate layer under an ocean, desert, plains, or any other biome has the same diamond density. Only Y-level matters.

Q: Is it worth mining diamond ore with Silk Touch?

A: Yes, for inventory management. Diamond ore blocks stack to 64; diamonds stack to 64 but each ore with Fortune III yields 2+ diamonds. Silk Touch lets you carry more potential diamonds per inventory slot. Mine with Silk Touch, Fortune them at base with Haste II beacon for maximum efficiency.

Q: Can I find diamonds in shipwrecks and village chests instead of mining?

A: Yes — diamonds appear in buried treasure chests, shipwreck treasure chests, village weaponsmith chests, jungle temple chests, desert temple chests, end city chests, and nether fortress chests. For a full diamond set (24 diamonds for armor + 2 for enchanting table + 3 for pickaxe = 29 total), exploration can supplement mining but is unlikely to replace it entirely.

This guide reflects diamond mining in Minecraft 1.18 through 1.21. The 1.18 ore distribution rework is the most significant change to diamond mining since Minecraft's release. Updated May 2026.
