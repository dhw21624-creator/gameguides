Quick Answer: Best Crosshair for Beginners

Use a cyan (#00FFFF) crosshair with no outlines, center dot on, inner lines 1-4-2-2 (opacity-length-thickness-offset), and movement/firing error off. This gives you a clean, visible crosshair that doesn't distract. Code: 0;P;c;5;o;1;d;1;0b;0;1b;0

Your crosshair is the single most personal setting in Valorant. There is no "objectively best" crosshair — the best crosshair is the one that lets you see heads clearly and makes you confident in your aim. But there are principles: colors that contrast with every map, sizes that don't obscure targets at range, and removing unnecessary visual clutter. This guide covers the theory, the settings, and community-sourced pro player codes.

Crosshair Color: The Most Important Setting

If enemies blend into your crosshair, you'll miss shots you should hit. The solution is choosing a color that doesn't appear on any Valorant map. Agent outlines are red by default, so avoid red crosshairs (they blend with enemies).

Pro Tip: Enable "Outlines" with opacity 0.5-1.0 and thickness 1. Outlines add a black border around your crosshair lines, making them visible on any surface regardless of color. This is especially useful for white and light-colored crosshairs.

Crosshair Style & Design

The default crosshair is clunky. Here's what the style settings do and what most players recommend:

SettingRecommendationWhy
Center DotOn (opacity 1, thickness 1-2)Gives a precise reference point for headshots. Without it, you're approximating center.
Inner LinesOn (opacity 1, length 4-6, thickness 1-2, offset 2-3)The crosshair "arms." Short arms keep the center clear; long arms provide peripheral reference. 1-4-2-2 is the default competitive starting point.
Outer LinesOff (or short: length 1-2 if you prefer)Adds visual clutter without improving precision. Most pros turn outer lines off entirely.
OutlinesOn (opacity 0.5-1.0, thickness 1)Ensures visibility against any background color.

Movement & Firing Error: On or Off?

Turn both OFF. Movement error makes your crosshair expand when you move, and firing error makes it expand when you shoot. While these give visual feedback on inaccuracy, they also obscure your view and train bad habits — you should KNOW when you're accurate without needing the crosshair to tell you. The sole exception: some new players benefit from movement error briefly (first 10-20 hours) to learn the timing of counter-strafing. Turn it off once you understand the mechanic.

How to Import Crosshair Codes

Valorant uses a crosshair profile system. Steps:

  1. Settings → Crosshair → Crosshair Profile → click the download icon (import profile code)
  2. Paste the code string and click Import
  3. The crosshair appears in your profile list. You can have multiple profiles and swap between them.

To export your crosshair: click the export icon next to your profile, and the code auto-copies to clipboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Should I copy a pro player's crosshair?

A: As a starting point, yes — it gives you a proven baseline. But tweak it to your preference. What works for TenZ (tiny white cross) might feel invisible to you. Copy a pro's crosshair, play 5-10 deathmatch games, then adjust color/size based on your experience.

Q: Is a dot crosshair better for headshots?

A: A dot forces you to be precise — if your dot isn't on their head, you miss. This is good for discipline but punishing when you're off. A small cross (short inner lines + dot) gives you both precision and peripheral reference. Try both and see which produces better headshot percentage.

Q: Do I need different crosshairs for different maps?

A: Some players use different crosshair colors for maps where their main color blends poorly (e.g., switching from green to cyan on Breeze). This is valid but adds complexity. Most pros use one crosshair for all maps. The crosshair profile system supports this if you want to set it up.

Q: Can I use crosshair overlays from third-party software?

A: Check Valorant's ToS. Monitor-level crosshair overlays (built into gaming monitors) are generally allowed. Software overlays that inject into the game are against ToS and may trigger Vanguard. When in doubt, use Valorant's built-in crosshair system.

Q: Why do some pros use a gap (offset) and others don't?

A: A gap/offset (inner line offset of 2-4) creates space between the crosshair lines and the center dot. This lets you see the enemy's head "framed" by the crosshair arms rather than covered by them. Close-range players often prefer no gap (offset 0); long-range aimers prefer offset 2-3. It's personal preference.
