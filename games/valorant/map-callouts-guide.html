Quick Answer: How to Give Good Callouts

  1. Location + Enemy + Damage: "Two A-main, one tagged 80" — not "They're over there!"
  2. Use the minimap zones: Valorant's minimap labels every area. Look at the zone name under your minimap and call it out.
  3. Short and loud: "Heaven" not "The elevated position overlooking the site." Callouts die if they take too long.
  4. Call out on death: The moment you die, tell your team where, how many, and how much damage you dealt. This is the most valuable comm you can give.
  5. Don't backseat: After giving info, shut up. Let your alive teammates use the info.

Callouts are the language of Valorant. Without them, your team is five individuals playing solo. With them, five players function as one unit with shared information. This guide covers the universal callout conventions, how to read and use the minimap, and map-by-map essential location names across the competitive map pool.

Callout Fundamentals

Good callout structure: [Number] [Location] [Detail]. Examples: "Two pushing A short, one has OP." / "Jett heaven, half HP." / "Spike down B site default." This structure gives your team actionable information they can immediately use.

Bad callouts to eliminate:

  • "They're right there!" — Where is "there"? Use location names.
  • "He's one HP!" — Was he actually 1 HP, or did you hit him for 120? Say "tagged 120" instead. "One HP" is the most lied-about callout in Valorant history.
  • "ALL A! ALL A!" — Are there actually 5 enemies A, or did you see 2 and panic? Call what you actually saw, not your fear.
  • Silence on death — The moment you die, you have perfect info. Share it.

How to Read the Minimap

Valorant's minimap is an information goldmine that most players underuse. Key features:

  • Zone labels: Hovering over any area on the minimap shows its official callout name. This is the name your teammates expect you to use. When in doubt, read the zone label.
  • Enemy markers: Red question marks appear where a teammate last saw an enemy. Red silhouettes appear on Cypher cam/Sova dart reveals. These decay quickly — call out the info before it disappears.
  • Sound indicators: White rings pulse on the minimap when enemies make noise (footsteps, abilities, gunfire). Even if your teammate didn't call it, you can see it on the minimap.
  • Spike icon: Shows spike carrier on attack; shows dropped spike location. Always track the spike.
Pro Tip: Bind your minimap zoom toggle. When you hear footsteps but don't know where, zoom out on the minimap to see the full map and spot the sound indicators. Zoom back in for normal play.

Map-by-Map Essential Callouts

Ascent

Key locations: A Main, A Lobby, A Heaven, A Hell, Tree, Garden, Mid Top, Mid Bottom (Market), Catwalk, Pizza, B Main, B Lobby, Boat House, CT, T Spawn.

Mid control: Ascent mid is the most contested area. Call "mid top" (attacker side, elevated) vs "mid bottom/market" (defender side, under catwalk). "Catwalk" is the elevated walkway connecting mid to A site — aggressive Omen/Jett players love peeking from here.

Bind

Key locations: A Short, A Lobby, A Site (default on triple boxes), Lamps/U-Hall, Showers, Hookah, B Long (Fountain), B Garden, B Elbow, Teleporters (TP to A / TP to B).

Unique call: Bind has teleporters — always specify WHICH teleporter. "He's TP" means nothing. "He TP'd from A short to B long" is actionable.

Haven

Key locations: A Long, A Short, A Heaven, A Sewer, B Site, B Window, C Long, C Garage, C Cubby, Mid Doors, Mid Window, Grass.

Three-site map: Always specify site AND sub-location. "A long" vs "A heaven" vs "A sewer" are three entirely different angles. Haven's defender rotations are long — calling the correct site early gives your teammates time to rotate.

Split

Key locations: A Main, A Lobby, A Ramp/Rafters, A Screens, A Heaven, B Main, B Garage, B Heaven, B Hell, Mid Mail, Mid Vents, Sewer.

Verticality: Split is the most vertical map. "Heaven" and "Hell" appear on both sites — specify "A heaven" or "B heaven." B site has multiple heaven angles (default heaven, back heaven, rafters).

Lotus

Key locations: A Main, A Lobby, A Tree, A Drop, B Site, B Upper, C Main, C Mound, C Waterfall, C Door, Mid.

Unique mechanics: Lotus has rotating doors and breakable walls. Call "A door broken" or "C door rotating" to alert teammates to route changes. The "A Drop" area is a one-way drop — once you go down, you can't go back up without rotating.

Additional maps (Abyss, Pearl, Fracture, Icebox, Breeze, Sunset): NEEDS VERIFICATION. Map pool rotates with patches. Core callout patterns (Heaven, Main, Long, Short, CT) apply universally. Read zone labels on the minimap for specific location names.

Communication Etiquette

  • Info only, no tilt: "Jett one-tapped me from heaven" is useful. "Jett is so lucky, this game is trash" is useless and tilts your team.
  • Don't repeat: If someone already called "two A main," you don't need to also say "two A main." Say "confirmed, two A main" only if you also see them.
  • Post-death info dump, then quiet: When you die, rapid-fire: location, number, damage, any utility used. Then stop talking. Your alive teammates need to hear footsteps, not your commentary.
  • Clutch time = mute time: If it's a 1v3 and your teammate is clutching, give info ONLY if you have NEW info they can't see on the minimap. Otherwise, stay silent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What's the easiest way to learn callouts?

A: Play the game and look at the minimap. Every time you enter a new area, glance at the zone label under the minimap. After 5-10 games on a map, the names become second nature. Custom games are also useful — load into a map alone and walk through each area, reading the labels.

Q: What if I don't know the name of a location?

A: Describe it quickly: "Behind the box on B site" or "Left side of A main entrance." Then look at the minimap after the round to learn the actual name. Don't stay silent just because you don't know the "official" name — any description is better than nothing.

Q: Do pro players use different callouts than ranked players?

A: Mostly the same, but pro teams develop team-specific callouts for micro-positions. For example, some teams number specific boxes or corners on site. In ranked, stick to the universal callouts everyone knows. Team-specific callouts only work if your whole team agrees on them.

Q: Should I call out damage on every fight?

A: Yes, especially when you die. "Tagged Reyna 120" tells your teammate Reyna is one body shot from death. "Tagged" + the number is the standard format. If you didn't see the damage number, "hit them" is better than nothing, but try to read the damage summary on your death screen.

Q: Is ping better than voice callouts?

A: Neither is "better" — they serve different purposes. Voice callouts carry complex information (how many enemies, what utility they used, damage dealt). Pings mark precise locations instantly. Use both: "Two pushing B main" (voice) + ping the exact corner they're in (ping). The combination is fastest for your teammates' reaction time.
