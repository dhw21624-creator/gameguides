Field Journal — The Nightlords of Limveld

Nightlord Weaknesses: Every Boss, Every Counter

Every expedition in Elden Ring Nightreign ends the same way: a showdown with a Nightlord on Day 3. Each of the eight Nightlords has a specific elemental weakness that you can check at the Roundtable Hold before you even set foot in Limveld. Matching your weapon element to that weakness is the single biggest damage increase available in the entire game. This guide breaks down every Nightlord, their weakness, and the exact tactics you need to bring them down — solo or in a squad.

I. The Complete Nightlord Weakness Table

Before any expedition, open your journal at the Roundtable Hold. Each Nightlord's weakness is displayed there. Memorize this table and you will never waste a run on the wrong element again.

The map marks locations with matching symbols — a Holy-marked ruin for Gladius, a Lightning-marked keep for Fulghor, and so on. Matching your element is worth more than any single stat point.

II. Marika's Churches: Your Flask Lifeline

Marika's Churches are the only source of flask charges in Nightreign. You start with very few, and each church you visit permanently increases your charge count for that run. This makes them the single most important objective on the map.

  • Rush them early: Churches sit on the outer edges of the map. Once the Rain arrives on Day 2, they become unreachable — and so do your extra flasks.
  • Plan your route: On Day 1, prioritize a path that sweeps two or three churches before you touch any hard content.
  • Coordinate in co-op: Split up in the opening minutes so each player grabs a different church, then regroup.

III. Nightlord-by-Nightlord Breakdown

Gladius, Beast of Night (Holy)

The first Nightlord you will face, and the game's tutorial for boss mechanics. Gladius is a three-headed beast whose attacks are heavily telegraphed. Bring a Holy weapon and you will melt his health bar. Learn his patterns here — every later Nightlord builds on the same fundamentals of spacing, stamina management, and stagger windows.

Fulghor, Champion of Nightglow (Lightning)

The easiest Nightlord for solo players. His Phase 1 attacks are slow and predictable, and he is also weak to Blood Loss — a Lightning weapon with bleed will trivialize the entire fight. Save your Ultimate Art for Phase 2, when his speed increases and his combos chain longer.

Caligo, Miasma of Night (Fire)

A fog-wreathed horror that punishes frontal approaches. Caligo's mist attacks deal heavy damage over time, so keep moving and use Fire weapons to burn through his miasma. In co-op, one player should kite while the other two focus on his exposed core during the stagger window.

Gnoster, Wisdom of Night (Fire)

A sentient pest swarm that overwhelms teams that stack together. Gnoster spawns adds throughout the fight — clear them fast or they will heal the boss. Fire damage burns through both the adds and the main body. Spread out to avoid the area attacks that wipe stacked squads.

Adel, Baron of Night (Poison)

A gaping-jawed horror that loves to grab. Adel's grab attacks are telegraphed by a long wind-up — dodge sideways, never backward. Poison weapons are the counter here, and they also help you manage the adds he summons. In solo, keep your distance and punish the grab whiffs.

Maris, Fathom of Night (Lightning)

The Augur — a boss that almost never enters melee range, making this a ranged fight. Maris fires sleep waves that put your whole party to sleep if not interrupted. The only way to counter the sleep wave is to attack aggressively and break the cast before it lands. Lightning weapons are the counter, but honestly, bring your strongest ranged option regardless of affinity. A team of two Ironeyes and a Recluse is ideal here.

Libra, Creature of Night (Madness)

The Equilibrious Beast is a balance-themed boss that punishes over-commitment. Madness weapons are the counter — they build up a status that staggers the beast out of its most dangerous combos. Watch your own Madness meter; standing in the boss's aura builds it fast, and hitting the threshold stuns you at the worst possible moment.

Heolstor, the Nightlord (Holy)

The final boss and the hardest fight in the game. Heolstor has two full health bars and one-hit-kill attacks at 75%, 50%, and 25% health. Holy weapons are the counter. Save your Ultimate Art for the second health bar, and never blow it early — most Nightlords reset their stagger bar on phase transition, and Heolstor is the worst offender. This is a war of attrition: land 1-2 hits per opening, dodge, and repeat.

IV. Solo Tactics: Beating Every Nightlord Alone

Solo mode scales enemy HP down to about one-third and boosts rune rewards by roughly 60%, but every death ends the run. These are the habits that separate solo survivors from solo wipes:

  • Enter the Nightlord fight at Level 13+ with a weapon at least Purple rarity and 6+ flask charges.
  • Equip Wending Grace — the self-revive consumable added specifically for solo players. One self-revive during the Nightlord fight is worth more than one stat point.
  • Apply the boss-weakness grease before the fight, plus Boiled Crab (20% damage negation) and Exalted Flesh (20% physical attack) if you have them.
  • Roll INTO attacks, not away. Rolling backward keeps you inside the hitbox longer. Nightreign bosses are designed to roll-catch players who panic-dodge early.
  • Use consumables aggressively. In a roguelike, hoarding items for later means dying with a full inventory. Your four item slots should be: Wending Grace > Boiled Crab > Boss-weakness grease > Exalted Flesh.
  • Learn when to run. If your flasks are gone and the boss is at 60% HP, retreating to a Site of Grace and re-engaging beats dying.
Heolstor Warning: His one-hit-kill attacks trigger at 75%, 50%, and 25% health on both health bars. When you see the wind-up, stop attacking and focus entirely on dodging. One greedy combo is all it takes to end a perfect run.

V. Co-op Coordination Tips

In co-op, the Nightlord will occasionally fixate on one player. When that happens, the other two should damage the boss to attract its gaze and switch aggro. Everyone has the same level and flask count, so juggling the boss's attention gives everyone damage windows and healing opportunities. Unlike the base game, there are no summons to hold aggro forever — every player is real, and every player makes mistakes. If a teammate dies multiple times, they cannot be revived.

Final Tip: Knowledge is the strongest weapon in Nightreign. Check the weakness before you drop in, rush Marika's Churches, match your element, and never over-commit. The Nightlords are beatable — solo or with a squad — if you respect their patterns and come prepared.
