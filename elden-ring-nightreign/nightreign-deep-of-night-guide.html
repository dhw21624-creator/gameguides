Elden Ring Nightreign Deep of Night Guide

Category: Mode Guide • Updated: 2026-09-19 • Game: Elden Ring Nightreign

Deep of Night is Nightreign's free post-launch mode, and it does not play like an ordinary expedition. The mode reworks the expedition structure, raises enemy damage and health well above the base game's baseline, and gates a set of rewards that cannot be earned anywhere else. This guide explains exactly what changed, how the Nightlord reveal works now, and the route discipline that separates a completed run from a wipe on Day 2.

The critical change is the Nightlord reveal. You can no longer clear a run and then pick the boss you want to fight. The Nightlord is shown before you commit — on the character select screen in the lower-left corner and on the map — which means your Nightfarer, Relic setup and route should all be chosen with the boss already in mind.

Everdark Sovereign Nightlords

Deep of Night can spawn Everdark Sovereign Nightlord variants. These are harder versions of the standard bosses with expanded movesets and far less forgiving punish windows. Because the run's Nightlord is revealed at the start, you will know whether a Sovereign is coming before you commit, and you should treat that as a signal to reroute toward Relic upgrades rather than raw damage.

  • Plan the whole run around the boss: a Sovereign fight demands maxed-out Relic slots more than it demands a high-level weapon.
  • Check the reveal first: confirm the boss at character select before locking in your Nightfarer.
  • Accept the reroute: if a Sovereign is revealed, drop the greedier loot routes and prioritise upgrades.
First clear advice: pick Wylder, Guardian or Ironeye for your first Deep of Night attempt. These Nightfarers have the highest forgiveness margins, which matters far more than raw damage output when enemy stats are inflated.

Route Discipline: Castle and Evergaol

Before you enter a Castle or an Evergaol in Deep of Night, re-check two things: your gear, and the colours of your Relic slots. The mode's inflated enemy stats punish mismatched Relic colours far more harshly than the base expedition does, and a stack of off-colour Relics will cost you a run that looked comfortable on paper.

  1. Confirm your Relic slot colours match what your build actually needs.
  2. Upgrade your weapon before entering a Castle, not inside it.
  3. Bank any surface-level loot on the way rather than backtracking later.
  4. Do not enter an Evergaol with a spent ultimate; the fights are longer than they look.

Day 2 Boss and the Final Supply Stop

After the Day 2 boss falls, the Spirit Shelter becomes your last meaningful preparation point before the Nightlord. Treat the smithing table and the merchant there as mandatory stops rather than optional ones: upgrading your weapon and restocking consumables at that exact point is the difference between a realistic attempt and a formality.

  • Smithing table: spend upgrades here rather than saving them.
  • Merchant: restock healing and throwables; the Nightlord fight is long.
  • Do not skip: there is no further supply point once you commit.

Mode-Exclusive Rewards

Deep of Night drops a distinct reward set that cannot be obtained through standard expeditions. The Forsaken Hollows update also added its own separate Deep of Night track, so if you are farming a specific reward, confirm which track it belongs to before you spend a session on route optimisation.

Patch 1.03.2 and Balance

Patch 1.03.2 substantially buffed the weaker Nightfarers, adjusted Relics, and made targeted improvements to raid behaviour while fixing more than fifty reported issues. The practical consequence is that the gap between the strongest and weakest Nightfarers in Deep of Night is narrower than at launch, so you can build around the character you actually enjoy rather than the one that used to be mandatory.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I choose the Nightlord in Deep of Night?

A: No. The Nightlord is revealed at the start of the run on the character select screen and on the map, and you must build around it.

Q: Are Deep of Night rewards available elsewhere?

A: No. The mode's exclusive rewards can only be earned inside Deep of Night runs.

Q: Which Nightfarer should I use for my first clear?

A: Wylder, Guardian or Ironeye, because their forgiveness margins suit the mode's inflated enemy stats.

Q: What is the most common cause of failed runs?

A: Skipping the Spirit Shelter supply stop before the Nightlord, and entering Castles with mismatched Relic slot colours.

Conclusion

Deep of Night is a planning mode disguised as a combat mode. Read the Nightlord reveal before you lock in a character, treat Relic colours as a hard requirement rather than a preference, and never walk past the final Spirit Shelter stop. Do those three things and the inflated enemy stats stop being the reason your runs end.
