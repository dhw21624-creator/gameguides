Black Myth Wukong Wan Yao Chao Zong DLC Guide: New Bosses & Extreme Difficulty

Category: DLC Guide • Updated: 2026-10-09 • Game: Black Myth Wukong

Black Myth Wukong's anniversary expansion, Wan Yao Chao Zong ("All Demons Face the Court"), launched in 2026 and gives the Destined One a whole new chapter. The DLC introduces a roughly 15-hour story arc centered on the Seven Sages, seven major new bosses, a dozen side quests, new weapons and talismans, and a brutal new Extreme difficulty mode built around the Resentment mechanic. This guide breaks down everything the expansion adds and how to prepare for its hardest fights.

Short version: Wan Yao Chao Zong is a full-size expansion — ~15 hours, 7 new bosses (including a hidden combined Four Monkeys fight), 12 side quests, and an Extreme difficulty that rewards counters and punishes button mashing.

The Story: The Seven Sages Arc

The expansion continues immediately after the base game's ending, centering on the Seven Sages — ancient demons who gather to "face the court" in a confrontation that reshapes the celestial and demonic realms. You play through a new story arc that connects to the Journey to the West mythology the base game draws from, with the DLC's opening questline establishing why the Seven Sages have resurfaced and how the Destined One is drawn into their war.

The campaign is substantial for a DLC: expect roughly 15 hours for a complete run, including side content. The writing leans into the series' blend of myth, tragedy, and martial-arts spectacle, and the expansion ends on a note that sets up the franchise's future.

New Bosses: The Ape Brothers, the Four Monkeys & More

The DLC adds 7 new bosses on top of the base game roster. The most talked-about encounters are the Ape brothers, who appear in full forms during the story, and the hidden combined "Four Monkeys" boss — a multi-phase secret fight that only appears if you complete specific side quest conditions. Community mechanics coverage highlights the Four Monkeys as one of the most demanding fights the genre has seen, with phases that force constant weapon and stance switching.

Beyond the marquee fights, the remaining bosses span different archetypes: humanoid duelists, giant beasts, and a demonic siege encounter. Each new boss has distinct tells, and the DLC expects you to learn them rather than relying on brute force — especially on Extreme difficulty.

Side Quests & New Gear

Twelve side quests expand the Seven Sages arc, offering lore, optional bosses, and gear. New weapons and talismans include the Earth Banner, which anchors you against knockback and empowers ground combos, and the Water Repellent Bead, a talisman that reduces damage from water-element attacks while improving dodge recovery. Several side quests gate the hidden Four Monkeys fight, so completing them is a priority for completionists.

Extreme Difficulty & the Resentment Mechanic

The headline addition for challenge players is the new Extreme difficulty setting, built around the Resentment (Yaoyuan) mechanic. In Extreme mode, enemies accumulate resentment stacks as combat continues. At 10 stacks they enter a berserk state with +80% attack and +50% attack speed, turning even trash mobs into threats if you let fights drag.

Resentment changes the pacing of combat entirely: you cannot safely kite or stall. You need to end engagements quickly, chain combos aggressively, and use finishers to reset enemy stacks where possible. Long, attritional fights are actively punished, which forces a much more offensive playstyle than the base game's defensive options.

Combat Tuning in Extreme Mode

Extreme difficulty also retunes your defensive tools. Dodge invincibility frames are shortened to 0.15 seconds, so panic-rolling through attacks no longer works — you must dodge with intent and timing. To balance this, the parry window is widened by 20%, a deliberate incentive to play the counter game. If you master parries, Extreme mode becomes a rhythm of punishes rather than a slog; if you rely on dodges, the shortened i-frames will punish you.

Key change: In Extreme difficulty, parry is your primary defensive tool. The widened window rewards counter-play, while dodge i-frames are too tight to carry you through fights.

Build & Tech Tips: No-Mod Zero-Investment Builds

One of the community's favorite discoveries is that no-mod, zero-investment builds are viable in the DLC. Because the expansion's talismans and weapons are strong on their own, players have cleared substantial content without respeccing into a heavy meta build — good news if you are returning after a long break and want to jump straight into the expansion.

For no-hit attempts, the community has mapped boss formulas for the marquee fights:

  • Ape brothers: bait the slower brother's overhead slam, parry it, then punish the faster brother during his recovery. Keeping both brothers on screen is safer than focusing one down and eating the other's attacks.
  • Centipede Demon: stay in front and react to the pincer telegraph — the hitbox is larger than it looks. The Water Repellent Bead significantly reduces the pain of its water-element follow-ups.

Achievement hunters should know that the expansion includes an achievement for a no-hit clear of the demon lair, the DLC's gauntlet-style final area. Even if you do not normally chase no-hit runs, the demon lair is short enough that a focused attempt is realistic.

How to Start & What to Bring

To start Wan Yao Chao Zong, you need a base-game save that has reached the endgame (the DLC entry point appears after the final main-story boss). You can bring your existing endgame character, and the expansion scales appropriately for New Game+ players. Recommendations before you start:

  1. Finish any base-game achievements you care about first — the DLC assumes a fully upgraded Destined One.
  2. Stock up on curatives and upgrade materials; Extreme mode consumes resources quickly.
  3. Re-familiarize yourself with parry timing. The base game may have let you dodge through everything; the DLC will not.
  4. Pick up the Earth Banner early — its knockback resistance makes several side-quest bosses dramatically easier.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long is the Wan Yao Chao Zong DLC?

A: Roughly 15 hours for a full run including side quests; longer if you chase the hidden Four Monkeys boss and no-hit achievements.

Q: Is Extreme difficulty worth playing?

A: If you enjoy the parry/counter game, yes — the widened parry window makes it one of the best implementations of the mechanic. If you relied on dodge i-frames in the base game, expect a brutal adjustment.

Q: How do I unlock the Four Monkeys boss?

A: It is hidden behind a set of side quest conditions. Follow all 12 side quests and pay attention to questline cross-references to unlock the combined fight.

Q: Do I need a new build for the DLC?

A: No. Zero-investment builds are viable thanks to strong new talismans and weapons, though dedicated builds still clear faster on Extreme difficulty.

Conclusion

Wan Yao Chao Zong delivers a true expansion-sized experience: a mythic story, seven demanding bosses, and an Extreme difficulty that redefines the combat loop around parries and aggression. Whether you are chasing the Four Monkeys secret or a no-hit lair clear, the DLC gives Black Myth Wukong a worthy second act.
